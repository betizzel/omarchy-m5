# For the Aurora Silicon folks (and anyone picking this up)

A curated front door, so you don't have to read a thousand lines of working
notes to find the signal.

**What this is:** live bring-up of Linux on a *real* M5 Pro — T6050, Mac17,9 /
J714s — dual-booted beside macOS. Single-core Arch boots from the internal SSD
today; SMP is the open blocker.

## The 3 things actually worth your time

1. **[`docs/smp-t6050-findings.md`](docs/smp-t6050-findings.md) — the top ~100
   lines** (the *"Strategic reframe"* and *"What SPTM does with a secondary CPU"*
   sections). The conclusion: on current macOS the blocker is **SPTM**. It owns
   secondary-CPU bring-up, so the classic "boot macOS under m1n1 and trace it"
   method is dead on T6050, and a secondary must be registered through SPTM
   (`sptm_register_cpu`, disassembly-confirmed from the T6050 SPTM binary). This
   is the part that saves you from re-running dead experiments.
2. **[`dt/`](dt/) — the T6050 device trees, authored from this machine's real
   ADT.** Hard data you can't get without the hardware. `t6050-pmgr.dtsi`
   (405 power domains) is generated by `scripts/adt2pmgr.py`.
3. **[`patches/`](patches/) — fixes that actually work on a real T6050:** the
   m1n1 USB-on-T6050 fix (`patches/m1n1/0005`), plus the Linux NVMe / SART /
   DockChannel keyboard+trackpad / PMGR patches.

## What we uniquely have

The running silicon. Static-only tracks — e.g.
[antiapplefox/m5-linux](https://github.com/antiapplefox/m5-linux), which nailed
the SPTM *boot-handoff* protocol — explicitly list M5 MIDR part IDs as
"unknown, need real hardware." We have it: real MIDR/PMGR values, the live ADT,
and on-metal m1n1 fixes. Our half and theirs are complementary.

## What we proved does **not** work

Booting macOS as a guest under m1n1 to trace it — SPTM won't let a hypervisor
observe macOS on T6050. The full autopsy (the GXF / `AVNCR_EL2` wall) is in the
lower half of the findings doc if you want it; otherwise skip it.
